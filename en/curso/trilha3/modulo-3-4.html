🔍 Audit Your Day — Predictable Triggers

Before you build, observe. A good Jarvis starts with a audit: which events in your day are predictable and always trigger the same work? “An email of type X arrived,” “every Monday at 9 a.m.,” “a new lead entered the CRM.” Every predictable trigger is a candidate to become part of the system.

🗺️ Map it in 3 steps

  1. 1List the recurring events of the week — what always happens and always prompts the same response.
  2. 2Mark the predictable — those with a clear start signal (an email arrived, the time came, a record was added).
  3. 3Choose 1 trigger with real pain points and low ambiguity to be the first to automate.

🔁 Triggered by You × Runs on Its Own

There is a huge difference between an AI that you trigger (open the chat, paste it, ask) and one that triggers on its own (the trigger fires, it works). The first still depends on your time. The real leverage is in the second: what runs while you sleep, serve a client, or are in a meeting.

✗ Triggered by you

  • ✗Only runs when you remember to open it.
  • ✗Consumes your time every time you use it.
  • ✗Stops existing when you leave.

✓ Runs automatically

  • ✓The trigger activates — without you there.
  • ✓Runs while you sleep or serve customers.
  • ✓Multiply your output without multiplying your hours.

⚖️ The real leverage

An automation you need to trigger is a tool. One that runs on its own becomes ability: works alongside you. That’s the leap from “I use AI” to “I have a Jarvis.”

🔒 Air-Tight: What Can Go Wrong Outside the Loop

The ability to run on its own comes at a price: you get out of the loop. When no one is watching every step, a small mistake goes straight to the customer. That’s why an always-on system needs to be air-tight — fail-proof, tested, and with safeguards—before you let it run free.

⚠️ Before you step away

  • !Test with real cases and edge cases—not just the happy path.
  • !Define what the system cannot do on its own (send it for human approval).
  • !Have a safeguard: an alert when something deviates from expectations, and a log of what was done.

🤫 Systems That Run Quietly in the Background

The Jarvises that win aren’t the loudest. They don’t have 100 tools or 50 integrations to impress. They run quiet in the background, cost little and do real work — every day, without drawing attention. Simplicity that works beats sophistication that breaks.

💡 Practical tip

Start with ONE system that solves ONE real pain point, runs cheaply, and you trust. One reliable Jarvis that does one thing well is worth more than ten fragile automations you have to babysit. Add capabilities only after the foundation is solid.
