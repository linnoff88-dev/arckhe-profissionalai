💬 Level 1 — Prompt

The foundation: how you ask. A few years ago, “prompt engineering” was everything — assigning a role, giving clear instructions, defining the end state, providing examples, and specifying what to do and what not to do. It’s still useful, but it matters less: models have become good enough on their own. Knowing how to ask well is the floor, not the ceiling.

🧱 What still matters in the prompt

  • •Role + objective + desired end state.
  • •Examples of what’s good (and what isn’t).
  • •Clear constraints—what to avoid.

🎛️ Level 2 — Vibe Code

Moving up a level: build by conversing. Vibe coding means describing what you want and iterating with AI until it exists—a little tool, an automation, a prototype. You don’t need to program everything from scratch; you need to direct it. This is where "making," not just "asking for text," begins.

✓ Good use

  • ✓A fast, ugly prototype to validate the idea.
  • ✓Automate a repetitive personal task.
  • ✓Iterate: watch it break, fix it, improve it.

✗ Trap

  • ✗Accepting code you don’t understand in production.
  • ✗Skipping validation because "it worked the first time".
  • ✗Confusing a prototype with a reliable system.

🧩 Level 3 — Context

Here’s the next step durable. A prompt is how you ask; context is what the AI knows — what’s in your head, your business, your schedule. No matter how good the models get, they still need what only you have. Module 3.2 goes deeper into context engineering.

📊 Why context beats prompts

If everyone uses the same model and asks for the same thing, the outputs all look alike. The your context — your knowledge, your history, your IP — that's what makes the result unique. Garbage in, garbage out.

🦾 Level 4 — Agentic

When AI stops just answering and starts act — using tools, taking steps, completing tasks—you’re at the agentic level. It’s powerful and risky: agents are “slot machines,” not “vending machines.” Module 3.3 covers when to use an agent and when a simple workflow is enough.

💡 Practical tip

Not everything needs an agent. If the task is deterministic ("every day at 9 a.m., pull the revenue and post it in Slack"), a simple workflow gets the job done more cheaply and breaks less often. Save the agent for tasks that are ambiguous and require reasoning.

🏛️ Level 5 — Architecture: the intention that matters

At the top, you stop thinking about messages and start designing the system: what context goes in, what rules apply, what goals to pursue, how to validate, and when a human decides. It’s the intent architecture — what matters isn’t the phrase, it’s the intent you encode in the system. That’s where Track 3 leads: from prompt to your Jarvis.

🧭 The pillars of the architecture of intent

  • •Context: what the system knows about you and the world.
  • •Rules: what it can and can’t do.
  • •Objectives: the "done" metric.
  • •Validation: how you check it before it reaches the client.
