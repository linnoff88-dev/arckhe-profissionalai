🎨 Taste & Judgment

As AI improves, it’s tempting to accept the first result as “good enough.” What sets you apart is the taste: knowing what deserves your name. AI generates the work; your judgment decides what goes out with your signature.

⚠️ The trap of trusting the first output

When you start using AI, you review everything: you read every word, check every claim. But the results get good, you relax—and fall into the trap. There’s a joke that sums up the risk: one person uses AI to turn a bullet point into a long, structured email; on the other end, the team uses AI to turn that email back into a bullet point. Everyone transforming, no one reading.

🧠 The central idea

The better the AI, the more dangerous it is to let your guard down. The standard is what keeps “good enough” from becoming “no one really read it.”

  • •At first, you review everything; over time, you trust too much.
  • •The first version is rarely the best—just the fastest.
  • •Using AI to write is great; accepting its output without reading it is the mistake.

— The "em dash" signal

Sometimes the sign of “this is AI” is small—like the em dash. AI fills everything with em dashes because it was trained on formal texts. If a colleague who knows you gets a text from you with five em dashes, they think: "So-and-so didn't write this; it's AI". And the moment you think this, it changes how you interpret the entire message — "did it read it? Is it all true?".

🏆 Study the best in your field

Taste is built through reference. If you work in sales, study great sales emails. If you work in marketing, study excellent landing pages. You can only recognize "great" in AI output if you know what great looks like in your field.

✓ What to DO

  • ✓Collect examples you consider excellent.
  • ✓Ask “why is this good?”—clarity? confidence?
  • ✓Compare the AI output against this benchmark.

✗ What NOT to do

  • ✗Copy and paste what you liked without understanding why.
  • ✗Thinking "any text" from AI is ready to use.
  • ✗Confusing formal with good.

🔄 Feed the Corrections Back In

The feedback loop is for the good e for the bad. When the AI writes something and you change five things, say: "I changed these 5 points, and here’s why". Update your instructions so it’s closer next time. That’s how you train the system to understand your taste.

💡 Practical tip

Keep a “style rules” file (in the project/CLAUDE.md/GPT instructions). Turn every recurring correction into a rule. Within weeks, AI will deliver in your tone by default.

📝 Example of structured feedback

1. Removed the em dashes → use a comma or period.
2. I cut “it’s important to note that” → get straight to the point.
3. I shortened paragraphs → max. 3 lines.
4. I replaced jargon with simple words.
5. I started with the benefit, not the context.

✍️ Your name is on the work

In the end, AI generates the work — your taste decides what deserves your name. If you produce something with AI, your signature is on it. If it’s great, you get the credit. If it’s bad, you get the blame. It doesn’t matter who wrote it: your name is on it.

🖋️ The subscription rule

Before sending anything made with AI, ask: "Would I feel comfortable putting my name on this?" If you hesitate, the criteria haven’t finished doing their job.
