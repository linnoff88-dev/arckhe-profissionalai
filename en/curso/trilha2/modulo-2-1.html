🩺 Diagnosis: The AI-Native Version of Your Profession

Before automating, map. Rushing to "put AI everywhere" wastes energy in the wrong places. The assessment answers a simple question: where does AI add more value in YOUR role—and what’s the first worthwhile target to tackle.

🗂️ Map Your Tasks and How Long Each Takes

You can’t optimize what you’ve never taken a hard look at. The assessment starts with an honest list: what do you actually do in a week? For a few days, write down each task and how long it takes. What repeats shows up on its own — and that's where AI has the most to gain.

1

List everything from one week

Emails, reports, meetings, spreadsheets, customer support. Nothing is too small.

2

Write down how long each one takes

Minutes per day or hours per week — a rough estimate is enough.

3

Mark what repeats

A task that comes back every week in the same format is the top candidate.

✂️ Separate: human judgment × repeatable work

With the list in hand, apply a filter: what do only you do because it requires judgment — context, relationships, decisions — and what’s mechanical and always the same? Repetitive work is where AI shines. The judgment stays yours; AI speeds up the work around it, not replaces it.

✓ Human judgment

  • ✓Choose the right tone for a difficult client.
  • ✓Prioritize what matters when everything is urgent.
  • ✓Read the context and relationship behind a request.

✗ Repeatable / mechanical

  • ✗Turn bullets into a structured email.
  • ✗Summarize a meeting or a long document.
  • ✗Fill out the same report template every week.

🎯 Where AI adds the most value in your role

Today’s AI is especially strong at a handful of actions that show up in almost every profession: write, analyze, summarize, draft, and search. Look at your list of repeatable tasks and mark which ones fall into these categories — that’s where the first gains show up fastest.

🧠 The five ways AI adds value

No matter your profession, these verbs keep recurring — and they’re exactly where AI speeds up the work.

  • •Write: emails, proposals, posts, descriptions.
  • •Analyze: find patterns in data, spreadsheets, feedback.
  • •Summarize: meetings, documents, long threads.
  • •Draft: the first version of anything.
  • •Search: gather and organize scattered information.

🥇 Choose Your First Target

Resist the urge to tackle everything. Choose a task: the one with a clear pain point, that repeats, and sits in the winning quadrant. One target helps you learn quickly, measure for real, and have a win to show—instead of five half-finished projects.

💡 Practical tip

Complete the sentence: "Every week I spend ___ hours on ___, and it's always the same." If you can fill it in without much thought, you've found your first target. Start small—one task, not a department.

🏁 Define “done” — your North Star metric

Diagnosis without a metric becomes opinion. Before you start, tie the target to a number: time spent, tickets resolved, proposals sent. This is your North Star—it tells you whether AI truly helped and gives you proof to show.

📐 What a good metric looks like

Choose your target and write the goal as a before-and-after number. Examples:

• Weekly report: from 3h → goal 1h
• Tickets/day: from 20 → goal 30
• Proposals/week: from 2 → goal 5
