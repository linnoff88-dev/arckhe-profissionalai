🌊 Every wave reshapes work

There’s a pattern that repeats with every major technological leap: technology changes the tasks of a profession long before changing the profession itself. A good professional doesn’t disappear — they adapts, adopts the new tool and starts delivering more. Those who don't move are the ones who get left behind. AI is just the latest wave in that same pattern.

🧠 The pattern of every wave

Notice the mechanics: the tool arrives, some of the manual work goes away, and what remains requires more judgment. The profession doesn't disappear—it gets redesigned from within.

  • •Technology takes away mechanical work and gives you time back for what matters.
  • •The profession survives; what changes is the to-do list of the person doing it.
  • •Early adopters become the go-to experts; those who refuse compete with people who have already advanced.

📑 The Spreadsheet Case: The Accountant and Excel

A spreadsheet is the perfect example. The VisiCalc (1979), o Lotus 1-2-3 (1983) e o Excel (1985) automated the calculation that used to be done by hand, cell by cell. The fear was obvious: "this will put accountants out of work." It didn't. The spreadsheet redefined the profession — those who adopted it multiplied their own output and moved on to analysis, not just calculations.

✓ Who ADOPTED the spreadsheet

  • ✓You multiplied your output: more clients, more scenarios, same team.
  • ✓Leveled up: became a decision consultant, not a number cruncher.
  • ✓Charged more for delivering analysis and projections, not just closing.

✗ Who REJECTED the spreadsheet

  • ✗Kept calculating by hand, more slowly and at greater cost.
  • ✗Lost work to colleagues who delivered twice as much in the same time.
  • ✗You got stuck on mechanical tasks while the market called for analysis.

💡 The lesson that stands the test of time

The spreadsheet didn’t eliminate the accountant — eliminated the advantage of not using spreadsheets. The tool has become the new baseline. Today, no one hires an accountant who refuses to use Excel. Remember this: that’s exactly what will happen with AI.

🌐 Internet and mobile: recent waves

The same pattern repeated with the internet and then with the mobile. The web created jobs that didn’t exist — SEO specialist, e-commerce manager — and reshaped marketing, sales, and customer service. The iPhone (2007) ushered in the era of apps and social media, and with it roles like "social media manager," which seem obvious today but were unthinkable not long before.

1

The web arrives (the 90s–2000s)

Websites, search, and e-commerce become business channels. SEO is born; marketing and sales reinvent themselves around digital.

2

The iPhone and apps (2007+)

The smartphone puts the internet in your pocket. Apps and social networks take off; every company needs a mobile presence.

3

New roles emerge

"Social media manager," growth, paid traffic manager: roles that didn't exist become central to the market.

💡 Practical tip

Notice the gap: the "social media manager" role only made sense after that the technology has arrived. Those who got in early on these waves set the rules. AI is at exactly that moment—AI-native roles are still being invented, and you can be the one to define them.

🚄 What AI Changes in a Different Way

AI follows the same pattern as previous waves—it’s like Excel, "but much bigger". The difference lies in the scale and in the speed. The spreadsheet mainly affected people who worked with numbers; the internet affected those who depended on digital channels. AI affects every vertical — text, images, code, customer service, legal, healthcare — and it’s arriving much faster than any previous wave.

⚖️ Tasks that move up × down

Within each profession, AI doesn’t treat every task the same. The repetitive and mechanical tasks go down — the machine takes over. In turn, the value rises for what only you do well: judgment, context, and human connection. The question is no longer “will AI replace me?” but “which of my tasks can it take on so I can focus on the ones that move me up?”

✓ Tasks that INCREASE in value

  • ✓Judgment: decide what matters, choose between options, take risks.
  • ✓Context: understand the customer, the company, what’s left unsaid.
  • ✓Relationship: trust, negotiation, care — the human side.

✗ Tasks that DECLINE in value

  • ✗Repetitive, standardized tasks done on autopilot.
  • ✗Transcription, formatting, and manual data organization.
  • ✗Generic first drafts that AI generates in seconds.

💡 Practical tip

Look at your week and classify each task as “moves up” or “moves down.” Tasks that move down are candidates for delegating to AI; tasks that move up are where you focus your time and reputation. This exercise is the starting point for Track 2.
