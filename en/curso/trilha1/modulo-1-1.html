🚀 AI Adoption Has Exploded — From Curiosity to the Norm

AI has moved off the “future trend” shelf and become standard business practice. According to Stanford AI Index 2025, 78% of organizations used AI in 2024—up from 55% in 2023. The leap in a single year shows that adoption has moved beyond isolated curiosity and become operational norm.

It’s not just “having AI somewhere”: the McKinsey (State of AI, 2024) indicates that about 65% of organizations already use generative AI regularly in at least one function — almost twice as many as the year before. The curve is steep, and anyone still watching from the sidelines is delaying their own baseline.

👔 What CEOs are saying — every leader becomes technical

Company leaders have already internalized the shift. According to the IBM CEO study cited in the source material, 85% of CEOs say that every functional leader needs to become a technology expert in their own domain. It’s not just the IT team: marketing, sales, finance, legal, and operations — everyone.

🧠 The central idea

Expectations have shifted from “there should be an AI team” to “every leader should master AI in their own area.” Technological fluency is no longer a niche advantage; it’s a job requirement.

  • •AI expertise is expected within from each specialty, not outsourced.
  • •Those who combine professional expertise + AI fluency get to the front of the line.
  • •The CEOs’ message is clear: the bar has risen for everyone, not just engineers.

📊 Jobs: what changes by 2030 — created × eliminated

The question isn’t "will jobs disappear?"—it’s "which tasks will change?". The WEF Future of Jobs 2025 (Jan/2025) projects 170M jobs created and 92M eliminated by 2030 — a net balance of +78M. But the number that matters most to you is another one: 39% of the key skills will change during this period.

There is also the exposure estimate: the Goldman Sachs (2023) projects that generative AI could expose the equivalent of 300 million of full-time jobs exposed to automation. "Exposure" is not the same as "elimination" — it means a significant share of tasks can be automated, creating room for those who reconfigure their own work first.

⚡ The real productivity gain—studies with numbers

It’s not a promise—it’s measured. Three independent studies point in the same direction: AI speeds up work and, in many cases, improves quality. And there’s a clear pattern: newcomers gain more than veterans, because AI levels the knowledge base.

1

Customer support: +14% (and +34% for newcomers)

Average productivity rose 14%, with a 34% jump among less experienced agents. Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (NBER, 2023).

2

GitHub Copilot: task 55% faster

Developers completed a coding task 55% faster with the assistant. GitHub Copilot.

3

Consulting: +12% tasks, 25% faster, +40% quality

In tasks within AI’s “frontier.” BCG × Harvard/Wharton (2023).

💡 Practical reading

The gains show up within the boundary that AI is good at — and the effect is greater for those just starting out. In other words: AI is the fastest lever for you to level up in your task, not for the company to “cut people.”

📚 Skills in Demand × Skills Losing Demand — What the Market Wants

If 39% of skills are changing, it’s worth knowing which way the balance is tipping. The WEF 2025 separates what's trending (growing demand) from what's losing relative weight. The good news: the trending skills are, for the most part, learnable — and many are human, not technical.

💡 Practical tip

"In decline" rarely means "extinct"—it's relative weight falling. The move is to shift your time from tasks AI can absorb to those that grow in value: judgment, creativity, and the ability to learn quickly.

⏳ Why the window is now open—the Excel advantage

Think of the accountant who learned Excel when spreadsheets arrived. For a while, he delivered in hours what his colleagues did in days—a a real, but temporary, advantage. When everyone mastered the tool, Excel became the floor, not the ceiling. It’s the same with AI: the advantage of adopting it early exists, and it has an expiration date.

Adoption: 55% (2023) → 78% (2024), Stanford AI Index 2025. The advantage of early adoption narrows as it becomes the norm—illustrative diagram.

💡 Practical tip

The advantage of being "the first in your circle" doesn’t last forever. The sooner you rebuild a real task with AI, the longer you benefit from the difference—before it becomes everyone’s baseline. Start now, with the smallest possible step.
